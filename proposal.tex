\section*{Abstract}
\textbf{Problem Definition:} Flight disruptions in air transportation rarely stay confined to their point of origin. In high-density airline networks, aircraft are assigned to cyclic multi-leg itineraries throughout the day. When an incoming flight lands late, that disruption either gets absorbed by the scheduled ground turnaround buffer or propagates into the next leg as a reactionary delay. Existing academic work on flight delays predominantly frames the problem as isolated binary classification (predicting whether individual flights exceed a 15-minute delay threshold), largely ignoring rotational tail-number dependencies and operational network effects. Conversely, operations research models that simulate delay trees often rely on single-machine solvers that cannot scale beyond single-airline samples or isolated single-day slices. 

To overcome these computational limitations, this project implements a distributed analytical pipeline in Apache Spark to reconstruct aircraft itineraries across more than 31 million domestic U.S. flight records from 2018 through 2022 ($\approx 9.8$ GB uncompressed). By computing chronological window functions over unique aircraft registrations (\texttt{Tail\_Number}), the pipeline quantifies how scheduled ground buffers absorb or amplify delays across different airport hubs and flight intervals. We benchmark distributed PySpark against a single-machine Pandas baseline to evaluate memory boundaries, execution runtimes, and partition efficiency on multi-gigabyte flight graphs.

\section{Project Aim and Motivation}
Aviation delays cost airlines and passengers tens of billions of dollars annually. While primary delays originate from local shocks like severe weather fronts, mechanical maintenance, or ground-crew shortages, secondary (reactionary) delays account for nearly half of all total delay minutes in mature airspaces. When a tight scheduled turnaround does not accommodate an incoming aircraft's late arrival, the subsequent departure is delayed even if downstream local weather and airspace conditions are completely clear.

This project addresses three operational and computational questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item What is the minimum scheduled turnaround buffer required at major hub airports to reliably absorb incoming arrival delays without triggering downstream departures?
    \item How do hub airports differ in their systemic resilience, distinguishing hubs that act as delay absorbers from those that function as delay amplifiers during peak traffic hours?
    \item What is the quantitative rate of delay propagation across consecutive flight legs, and how can these insights guide buffer scheduling?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Why Big Data Technologies Are Required:} Tracking aircraft rotations over five continuous years requires cleaning, sorting, and windowing tens of millions of records. A single narrow-body aircraft can perform four to eight legs daily, producing continuous operational chains across hundreds of airports. Reconstructing these sequences requires a distributed stateful window operation:
\begin{center}
\texttt{Window.partitionBy("Tail\_Number").orderBy("FlightDate", "CRSDepTime")}
\end{center}
Executing this operation on a 9.8 GB tabular dataset requires distributed hash-partitioning, secondary sorting, and join operations. On a single machine with 16 GB RAM, conventional tabular libraries (such as Pandas) crash with out-of-memory (OOM) errors or induce heavy operating system swap thrashing during grouping and lagging steps. Apache Spark overcomes this bottleneck by distributing aircraft partitions across worker nodes, spilling shuffles to disk when necessary, and leveraging the Catalyst query optimizer to prune unused columns before computation.

\section{Data}
The project uses the \textit{Flight Delay Dataset 2018--2022}, curated by Rob Mulla from official reporting records maintained by the U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics (BTS).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Source:} Published openly on Kaggle (\url{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/robikscube/flight-delay-dataset-20182022}), originating from the BTS Office of Airline Information (Form 41, Schedule T-100).
    \item \textbf{Type:} Tabular relational flight operational logs.
    \item \textbf{Structure and Volume:} The data spans five calendar years (2018 through 2022) across five primary files:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \texttt{Combined\_Flights\_2018.csv} (1.9 GB)
        \item \texttt{Combined\_Flights\_2019.csv} (2.7 GB)
        \item \texttt{Combined\_Flights\_2020.csv} (1.7 GB)
        \item \texttt{Combined\_Flights\_2021.csv} (2.1 GB)
        \item \texttt{Combined\_Flights\_2022.csv} (1.4 GB)
    \end{itemize}
    The uncompressed CSV files total approximately \textbf{9.8 GB} (well exceeding the 1 GB course minimum) and represent approximately 31.7 million flight records across 61 attributes. Snappy-compressed Parquet alternatives are available at $\approx 1.06$ GB total.
    \item \textbf{Key Features for Sequence Linking:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Identifiers: \texttt{Tail\_Number}, \texttt{FlightDate}, \texttt{Operating\_Airline}, \texttt{Flight\_Number\_Operating\_Airline}.
        \item Spatial endpoints: \texttt{Origin}, \texttt{Dest}, \texttt{OriginAirportID}, \texttt{DestAirportID}.
        \item Schedule and actual timestamps: \texttt{CRSDepTime}, \texttt{DepTime}, \texttt{CRSArrTime}, \texttt{ArrTime}.
        \item Delay metrics: \texttt{DepDelay}, \texttt{ArrDelay}, and causal breakdown fields (\texttt{LateAircraftDelay}, \texttt{CarrierDelay}, \texttt{WeatherDelay}, \texttt{NASDelay}).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chunking and Partitioning Strategy:} 
    Raw CSVs will be ingested into Spark, validated, and converted into Apache Parquet format. Data will be partitioned on disk by \texttt{Year} and \texttt{Month}. During the sequence linking phase, data frames will be repartitioned using a hash partitioner on \texttt{Tail\_Number} to ensure that all flights for a given airframe reside in memory on the same executor partition, eliminating redundant shuffles during sequential window operations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Tools and Frameworks}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Apache Spark (PySpark):} The backbone framework for distributed data ingestion, data hygiene checks, distributed joins, and windowed sequence transformations. Spark's DataFrame API and Catalyst engine optimize execution plans across multi-core executor nodes.
    \item \textbf{Apache Parquet:} High-performance columnar storage format with dictionary encoding and Snappy compression. It reduces disk footprint from 9.8 GB to $\approx 1.1$ GB and enables column pruning, drastically lowering I/O latency for window queries that only require timestamp, airport, and tail-number columns.
    \item \textbf{Pandas / DuckDB (Baseline):} Used as a single-node control benchmark on isolated data slices (1-month, 6-month, and 1-year slices) to empirically document where single-node architectures fail due to memory exhaustion.
    \item \textbf{Plotly and Matplotlib:} For generating turnaround buffer response curves, spatial delay propagation heatmaps, and scalability benchmark charts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Expected Outcomes and Evaluation}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Benchmarked Distributed Performance:} A rigorous comparison between single-node Pandas and distributed Apache Spark across varying data volumes (500 MB, 2 GB, 5 GB, and 9.8 GB). Evaluation will measure total elapsed execution time, peak memory usage, CPU core utilization, and speed-up ratios.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Turnaround Buffer Absorption Curves:} Quantifying the relationship between scheduled turnaround duration ($B_{sched} = \text{CRSDepTime}_{k+1} - \text{CRSArrTime}_k$) and net delay variation ($\Delta D = \text{DepDelay}_{k+1} - \text{ArrDelay}_k$). We will evaluate the empirical buffer threshold at which $\Delta D \le 0$ for top U.S. carriers.
    \item \textbf{Hub Resilience Taxonomy:} Categorization of major hub airports (e.g., ATL, ORD, DFW, DEN, JFK) into delay-absorbing versus delay-amplifying nodes, controlling for seasonal weather and airport congestion indices.
    \item \textbf{Operational Insights and Visualization Dashboard:} A set of data-driven heuristics indicating where schedule padding yields the greatest reduction in downstream reactionary delays.
\end{enumerate}
