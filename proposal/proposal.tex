\section*{Abstract}
\textbf{Problem Definition:} Commercial airliners operate cyclic multi-leg rotations throughout the day, creating direct operational dependencies between consecutive flight legs. Consequently, an inbound arrival delay threatens the scheduled departure of the subsequent leg unless absorbed during ground turnaround. Standard flight delay studies treat each flight as an independent observation and train basic classifiers for 15-minute delays, ignoring physical aircraft rotations. Conversely, network propagation models in operations research capture delay trees but rely on single-node solvers that collapse under multi-year national flight volumes. 

This project addresses this computational bottleneck by building an aircraft-tracking pipeline in Apache Spark over 31.7 million U.S. domestic flights spanning 2018 through 2022 ($\approx 9.8$ GB raw tabular data). Using distributed chronological windowing over unique aircraft registrations (\texttt{Tail\_Number}), the pipeline links consecutive legs under strict spatial continuity ($Dest_k = Origin_{k+1}$) and date-aware timestamp arithmetic. We evaluate observational delay absorption dynamics across major hubs and empirically benchmark distributed PySpark against a single-machine Pandas/DuckDB baseline across scaled data volumes.

\section{Project Aim and Motivation}
In mature airspace networks, secondary (reactionary) delays routinely exceed primary delay minutes during afternoon departure banks. When an aircraft arrives behind schedule, any deficit exceeding the scheduled ground buffer spills into subsequent departures, propagating disruptions across downstream airports regardless of local weather. While recent econometric and operational studies analyze scheduled ground buffers to mitigate reactionary delay \cite{wu2021}, evaluating adaptive buffer allocation policies across multi-year nationwide networks remains computationally challenging.

This project delivers two distinct contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Big Data Engineering Track:} We design a distributed PySpark architecture to clean, partition, and window ~32 million flight records. We evaluate the performance trade-offs between uncompressed CSV and columnar Parquet formats, evaluate hash-repartitioning by \texttt{Tail\_Number} versus default window shuffle, and conduct systematic benchmarks against single-machine baselines to identify the empirical crossover threshold where distributed scaling justifies cluster coordination overhead.
    \item \textbf{Aviation Operations Track:} We model the observational relationship between scheduled turnaround duration and downstream delay variation ($\Delta D = \text{DepDelay}_{k+1} - \text{ArrDelay}_k$). Through counterfactual scenario analysis, we compare uniform buffer schedules against airport- and time-adaptive alternatives, assessing the operational trade-off between delay risk and fleet utilization (idle ground time).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Why Big Data Technologies Are Required:} Tracking aircraft itineraries across five years requires stateful window operations across 31.7 million rows. A commercial airliner flies 4 to 8 legs daily. Chaining these legs requires a distributed window transformation:
\begin{center}
\texttt{Window.partitionBy("Tail\_Number").orderBy("FlightTimestamp")}
\end{center}
We hypothesize that single-machine in-memory grouping will face severe memory contention and swap paging on multi-million row rotation graphs. PySpark mitigates this via worker partition distribution, offloading shuffle partitions to disk under memory pressure, and pruning unreferenced attributes early through the Catalyst optimizer.

\section{Data}
The study uses the multi-year flight dataset curated by Rob Mulla on Kaggle (\url{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/robikscube/flight-delay-dataset-20182022}), derived from official U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics (BTS) Airline On-Time Performance reporting (14 CFR Part 234).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Type and Volume:} Five annual files (\texttt{Combined\_Flights\_2018} through \texttt{2022}) totaling \textbf{9.8 GB in raw CSV} ($\approx 31.7$ million records, 62 columns), exceeding the 1 GB course minimum. Snappy-compressed Parquet alternatives occupy $\approx 1.06$ GB total.
    \item \textbf{Core Features:} Airframe identifiers (\texttt{Tail\_Number}, \texttt{Operating\_Airline}), route endpoints (\texttt{Origin}, \texttt{Dest}), local timestamps (\texttt{CRSDepTime}, \texttt{DepTime}, \texttt{CRSArrTime}, \texttt{ArrTime}), and delay metrics (\texttt{DepDelay}, \texttt{ArrDelay}, \texttt{DepDel15}).
    \item \textbf{Leg Pairing and Feasibility Audit (2022 Data):} Consecutive flight pairs are linked under four explicit criteria: (1) matching \texttt{Tail\_Number}, (2) chronological ordering combining \texttt{FlightDate} with departure/arrival times to resolve cross-midnight turnarounds, (3) spatial continuity ($Dest_k = Origin_{k+1}$), and (4) exclusion of cancelled/diverted legs. In our preliminary audit of 4,078,318 records from 2022, 98.96\% of completed, same-day consecutive flights satisfied spatial continuity, confirming that rotation chains can be isolated from tail swaps and ferry repositioning flights.
    \item \textbf{Partitioning and Chunking Strategy:} Data is partitioned on disk by \texttt{Year} and \texttt{Month} in columnar Parquet format. In memory, we will evaluate hash-repartitioning by \texttt{Tail\_Number} and chronological windowing, measuring shuffle volume, execution time, and memory usage to determine their empirical impact on pipeline throughput.
\end{itemize}

\section{Tools and Frameworks}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Apache Spark (PySpark):} Distributed engine for ingestion, schema casting, hash-repartitioning, and chronological window transformations across multi-core workers.
    \item \textbf{Apache Parquet:} High-efficiency columnar storage with Snappy compression, reducing storage by $\approx 88\%$ and enabling predicate pushdown on \texttt{Origin} and \texttt{FlightDate}.
    \item \textbf{Single-Node Baseline (Pandas / DuckDB):} Used as a control benchmark on 500 MB, 2 GB, and 5 GB slices to quantify speed-up ratios and measure where single-node architectures saturate memory.
    \item \textbf{Plotly / Matplotlib:} Generation of buffer response curves, spatial delay propagation flows, and scalability benchmark profiles.
\end{itemize}

\section{Expected Outcomes and Evaluation}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Distributed Scalability Profiles:} Controlled benchmarks comparing single-node execution against PySpark across data scales (500 MB to 9.8 GB). Metrics include wall-clock runtime, peak heap consumption, shuffle volume, and speed-up curves.
    \item \textbf{Turnaround Buffer Association Models:} Empirical characterization of observational delay variation $\Delta D = \text{DepDelay}_{k+1} - \text{ArrDelay}_k$ against scheduled buffer $B_{sched}$. Findings will be reported as observational associations rather than causal effects, acknowledging unobserved operational factors like expedited boarding or gate changes.
    \item \textbf{Counterfactual Buffer Scenario Analysis:} Comparative evaluation of three scheduling policies: (a) uniform fixed buffers, (b) airport-specific buffers conditioned on historical congestion, and (c) dynamic airport-plus-hour buffers, assessing projected delay mitigation against added fleet ground idle time.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible Pipeline:} An open-source, documented PySpark repository with modular scripts for ingestion, sequence linking, and performance profiling.
\end{enumerate}
